The 10:15 to Porto left without me. Not because I was late — I was on the platform, coffee in hand — but because I'd gotten on the wrong train entirely. I realized this somewhere around Setúbal, watching the coastline unspool in the wrong direction.

## The Wrong Stop

I got off at a town I couldn't pronounce. The station had one bench, a faded schedule, and a cat who appeared to own the place. I sat down, opened my phone to reroute, and then put it away.

*Why not?*

I walked into town. It was Tuesday, which apparently means the fish market runs until noon. I made it by 11:40. I bought a bag of something grilled that the vendor pointed at with enthusiasm and I ate it standing up, watching the boats rock.

## The Meal I Didn't Plan

An older man at a nearby table waved me over. His English was four words; my Portuguese was two. We managed fine. He ordered for both of us — a stew that arrived in a clay pot, dark and deep, with bread that had clearly been made that morning.

I don't know what was in it. I ate three bowls.

---

I made it to Porto six hours later than planned. My Airbnb host had left a note: *Late arrivals welcome. Wine on counter.* I poured a glass and stood on the balcony watching the Douro catch the last of the light.

Getting lost, it turns out, is still one of the best ways to arrive somewhere.
