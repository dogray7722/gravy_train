I've been walking more lately. Not for exercise — I have a bike for that — but for the pace of it. Walking is slow enough that things can happen *at* you without being requested.

## The Speed We Chose

Moving fast is a personality trait we've collectively mistaken for a virtue. The people who respond to emails at 11pm are not more committed. They're just more available, which we've confused for the same thing.

I spent three years being very fast and very available and produced a lot of things I don't think about anymore.

## What Slowness Actually Costs

People will tell you slowness costs opportunities. This is sometimes true. What they won't mention is what speed costs: the ability to notice what's happening while it's happening. The capacity to finish a thought. The memory of how an afternoon felt.

*Speed is a trade.* Most of us made it without reading the contract.

## What I'm Doing Instead

I'm not trying to be slow for its own sake. I'm trying to be appropriately paced — fast when the thing calls for it, slow when it doesn't. The difficult part is learning to tell the difference, because urgency is contagious and usually invented.

I walk now. I finish books. I cook things that take time. I'm experimenting with not answering immediately and seeing what happens.

---

So far: nothing bad has happened. Several good things have.
