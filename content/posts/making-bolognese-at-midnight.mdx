The rule is: never cook bolognese sober. Not because the wine goes in the sauce — though it does, a lot of it — but because bolognese is a patience game, and patience at midnight requires a glass in hand.

## What's Actually in the Fridge

Half a red onion. Two carrots that were optimistic three days ago. A pound of ground beef I had to use tonight or lose. Half a bottle of something Italian I opened Wednesday. A can of whole tomatoes in the back that I forgot I had.

This is enough.

## The Only Instructions You Need

Brown the meat. Don't rush it. Let it go past where you think it needs to go — that color matters. Add the vegetables small, the wine large. Open the tomatoes with your hands, not a spoon. Low heat. An hour minimum. Two is better.

*Don't touch it.* This is where people go wrong. They stir, they adjust, they hover. Leave it alone. It knows what it's doing.

## Why This Matters at Midnight

There's something clarifying about cooking late. No performance. No planning. Just the smell of something slowly becoming what it's supposed to be.

I've made bolognese from recipes and I've made it like this. The midnight version is always better. Maybe it's the wine. Maybe it's the fact that nobody's watching.

Eat it straight from the pot. There's no shame in this.
