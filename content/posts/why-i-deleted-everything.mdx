It started with a notification I didn't ask for. Then another. Then I realized I'd spent forty minutes reading strangers argue about something I didn't care about two minutes before I opened the app.

## The Accumulation

Social media doesn't take from you all at once. It takes in increments so small you don't notice until you're hollow. A morning here. An evening there. The slow replacement of your actual opinions with reactions to other people's opinions about other people's opinions.

I kept a running note for a month: every time I opened an app out of boredom rather than intention. I filled two pages.

## What I Actually Missed

When I deleted the accounts — all of them, same day, no goodbye post — I expected to feel something dramatic. Withdrawal, maybe. Anxiety.

Instead I felt *quiet*.

Not the absence of noise. Actual quiet — the kind where your own thoughts have room to finish themselves before something interrupts. I started reading again. Not articles. Books. Long ones with slow middles.

---

I'm not telling you to delete anything. But I will say this: the version of yourself that existed before you were performing for an audience is still in there. You might want to check in on it sometime.
