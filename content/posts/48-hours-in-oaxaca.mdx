The flight landed at 6am. By 6:45 I was in a market eating a tlauyda the size of a steering wheel, standing up, jet-lagged and completely at peace with the world.

## Day One: The Learning Part

The mezcal situation takes about four hours to understand. There are categories. There are producers. There are agave varieties that matter in ways someone will explain to you with genuine conviction. By lunch I had a notebook full of words I couldn't pronounce and strong opinions about terroir I hadn't had that morning.

The mole took longer. I watched a woman in a restaurant make it for two hours before I sat down to eat. Twenty-seven ingredients. I stopped writing them down around eighteen.

## Day Two: The Part Where You Stop Trying

I had no plan for day two and it was the better day. I walked. I got pointed at a market nobody had told me about. I bought a piece of textile I can't afford to hang anywhere and a bag of chocolate I finished before the hotel.

A man named Rodrigo spent forty-five minutes explaining to me, with great patience, that the mezcal I'd been drinking the day before was perfectly fine but *this* one — gesturing to a bottle without a label — was what I should have been drinking.

He was right.

---

48 hours isn't enough. I already know I'm going back. Some places don't let you leave clean.
