Nobody tells you about the stairs. You read "perched on a cliff" and you imagine the view. You do not imagine 437 uneven stone steps between you and breakfast, carrying a bag you absolutely overpacked, in October heat that has no business being October.

By the third morning I had stopped counting steps. By the fifth I was taking them two at a time.

## Getting There

The ferry from Salerno is the correct choice. You arrive by sea, which is the only sensible way to arrive somewhere built into a cliff face. The town appears slowly — first the color, then the detail, then the specific chaos of boats and tourists and men shouting at each other about parking.

There's a hotel up a set of stairs that branches off another set of stairs. The man at the front desk asked how I'd heard about the place. I told him a friend had stayed there. He seemed satisfied with this and gave me a room with a terrace the size of a parking space and a view that made the stairs feel very small.

## The Food Situation

Eating on the Amalfi Coast is not complicated. You find a place with a terrace. You order whatever's written on a chalkboard. You eat it slowly because there is nowhere to be and you have now accepted this.

The pasta is always the right choice. The seafood is always the right choice. The limoncello at the end is mandatory in the sense that the waiter will simply bring it and you will simply drink it.

I ate at the same trattoria four of the five nights. On the third night the owner came and sat down uninvited and talked for twenty minutes about his opinion of a neighboring restaurant. I agreed with everything he said. It seemed like the right approach.

## The Drive to Ravello

You should drive the coastal road at least once. You should also accept, before you do, that the road is approximately one car wide, buses use it regularly, and the drop on one side is not something you want to look at directly.

I hired a driver for half a day. He drove with one hand and gestured with the other in a way that suggested both that the scenery was worth admiring and that he personally had transcended any concern about the geometry of the road.

Ravello is quieter. It sits up in the hills above the coast and has the feeling of a place that has mostly given up on tourists without being unfriendly about it. The gardens at Villa Cimbrone are worth the walk.

## A Note on Crowds

October is the right time to go. The summer crowds have thinned without the weather having turned. You can walk through the center of Positano in the morning without feeling like you're in a corridor.

By November the ferries run less often. By December, I'm told, some of the restaurants close and the town belongs to the locals for a few months. I think about that sometimes. I think I'd like to see it.

The stairs were worth it. They're always worth it.
